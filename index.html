Why this project exists

The ECU Problem Is No Longer Just Fueling

When fuel blend, temperature, load, and emissions limits all move at once, a static lookup-table ECU becomes a compromise. FlexMind turns calibration into a live decision problem.

Static MapsTraditional ECUs are calibrated for known conditions.

Dyno-built tables work when fuel is predictable — but India is moving through E10, E20, and standards beyond. A fixed map can't elegantly handle every blend, region, and driving style.

Blend VariabilityEthanol changes the combustion equation.

Higher ethanol means lower energy density, a different stoichiometric AFR, higher latent heat, and more octane headroom. The ECU must adjust without hurting drivability.

Emissions PressureNOx control fights efficiency.

Reducing NOx usually means EGR and conservative combustion temperatures — which can raise BSFC. Calibration is a trade-off, not a single-number target.

Live FeedbackThe vehicle already knows more than old maps use.

Load, pressure, temperature, blend, and stability signals can become a closed-loop calibration layer that chooses a cleaner, safer operating point.

India ethanol roadmap

E30 Is The Next Calibration Test

India's current transition centers on E20, but BIS has already notified fuel-quality standards for E22, E25, E27, and E30 — vehicles and ECUs need to be prepared.

01Lower energy per litre

E30 needs more fuel injected for the same torque demand — without compensation, economy and drivability feel inconsistent.

02Higher octane potential

More ethanol supports more knock resistance — room for cleaner timing and higher compression strategies.

03Cold-start sensitivity

Ethanol vaporizes differently, so startup enrichment and transient fueling matter more, especially in winter.

04Material compatibility

Fuel lines, seals, pumps, injectors, and tanks need validation as blend percentage rises.

05Live feedback matters

Blend, load, stability, and emissions feedback can update ECU strategy instead of one static map.

06Clear expectations

A phased rollout, model-year differences, and compatibility labels precede E30 becoming normal.

Research paper abstract

The Paper

Conventional ECUs rely on static calibration maps built from expensive dynamometer testing. FlexMind reframes the ECU as an adaptive decision layer: live parameters feed cross-validated surrogate models, and an optimizer chooses settings that balance efficiency and emissions.

Random Forest, Ridge, Gradient Boosting, and XGBoost are benchmarked head-to-head under five-fold held-out cross-validation to predict BSFC, NOx, and Gross Indicated Thermal Efficiency — with an explicit boundary study on Soot and CO emissions.

Methodology

The AI Pipeline, Step By Step

An engineering pipeline: ingest data, train surrogate models, validate rigorously, search ECU control space, and choose the best trade-off for the current engine state.

01
Data ingestion

Load, fuel shares, timing, pressure, EGR, boost, temperatures, cylinder pressure, duration, stability, and target outputs.

02
Preprocessing

Encode combustion mode, mean-impute missing values, isolate the thirteen-feature input matrix from targets.

03
Surrogate modelling

Train Random Forest, Ridge, Gradient Boosting, and XGBoost regressors per target — no physical dyno test per setting.

04
Validation

Five-fold held-out cross-validation with out-of-fold scoring — never trained-partition evaluation.

05
Optimization

Grid search over injection timing, fuel pressure, EGR rate, and boost pressure within feasible bounds.

06
Pareto decision

Weighted scalarization moves along the BSFC–NOx front for efficiency- or emissions-biased control.

Dataset anatomy

What The Model Actually Sees

Operating condition, fuel composition, ECU command, combustion feedback, and target outputs — made legible for non-coders.

Operating stateLoad, RPM context, temperature

Engine load percentage, intake temperature, and equivalence ratio describe the moment being calibrated for.

Fuel compositionDiesel / gasoline energy share

Blend ratio changes AFR targets, latent heat, and octane headroom available to the calibration.

ECU commandTiming, pressure, EGR, boost

Injection timing, fuel pressure, EGR rate, and boost pressure are the four control levers being searched.

Combustion feedbackPressure peak, duration, stability

Cylinder pressure peak, combustion duration, and stability index capture how the engine actually responded.

TargetsBSFC, NOx, thermal efficiency

The three genuinely learnable surrogate outputs under held-out cross-validation.

Boundary caseSoot, CO emissions

Evaluated identically but not predictable from this feature set — a finding, not a shortcoming.

Examiner questions

Questions This Project Is Ready To Answer

Why benchmark four regression models?

To show whether ensemble complexity earns its keep over a Ridge baseline — Ridge matches or slightly exceeds the ensembles here.

Why held-out cross-validation?

In-sample scores overstate generalization. Five-fold, out-of-fold scoring reflects genuine real-world accuracy.

Why multi-objective optimization?

Fuel efficiency, emissions, and stability are coupled — optimizing only BSFC can push emissions the wrong way.

What is still needed for deployment?

HIL testing, dyno validation, embedded inference optimization, and manufacturer-specific compatibility testing.

Project overview

About FlexMind AI ECU

FlexMind AI ECU is an automotive AI platform demonstrating adaptive ECU calibration using machine learning.

It uses surrogate models to predict BSFC, NOx, and Gross Indicated Thermal Efficiency while performing multi-objective optimization for flex-fuel and dual-fuel engines.

The research explores how AI can improve fuel efficiency, reduce emissions, and support trustworthy, adaptive ECU decision-making.
